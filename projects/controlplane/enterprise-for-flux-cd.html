04 · The problem · copy: data sheet challenges
The challenges we hear most
Where community Flux stops and regulated operations begin.
Unmanaged Flux lifecycle across environments
Upgrading and operating Flux across clusters requires manual coordination, creating version drift, prolonged exposure to unpatched vulnerabilities, and inconsistent configuration.
Compliance and regulatory gaps
Regulated environments require CVE remediation SLAs and supply chain attestation (SBOM, VEX, SLSA) beyond what community projects are designed to provide, leaving platform teams to bridge the gap manually.
Platform performance at scale
As cluster count and deployment frequency grow, reconciliation throughput becomes a bottleneck. Platform teams absorb escalations from slow deployments and degraded reliability.
Limited observability across fleets
No accessible view of reconciliation health in multi-cluster environments increases mean time to diagnosis and relies on manual investigation.
05 · What you get · copy: data sheet value set
What the distribution provides
Consistent, current Flux on every cluster
The Flux Operator automates installation, configuration and upgrades across fleets, removing version drift and reducing operational overhead. ResourceSets provide dynamic templating.
Hardened images with n-2 version support
Cryptographically signed container images, continuous vulnerability scanning, and backported security patches maintaining zero known CVEs, under guaranteed remediation SLAs.
Optimised platform performance
Tuned and benchmarked configurations reduce change latency; configuration errors surface early; notifications close developer feedback loops.
Observability UI and AI-assisted operations
A web UI provides visibility into reconciliation health, paired with an MCP server connecting AI assistants to Flux-managed clusters for agentic GitOps workflows.

Source conflicts and choices made
FIPS wording. The Flux data sheet says "FIPS-validated cryptography" in one challenge; the guidance reserves exact FIPS phrasing rules for OpenBao and describes Flux as "FIPS-ready". The page uses the data sheet’s own headline wording "FIPS 140-3 compliance" and avoids "validated". Flagged for sign-off.
Zero CVEs. "Zero known CVEs" is used because the Flux data sheet states it with its precise scope (current release and two previous minor versions, backported patches, SLAs); the "don’t sell zero CVEs alone" rule applies to the Echo partnership, not this product. Scope kept attached to the claim throughout.
Morgan Stanley scale figures. The strategy cites 500+ clusters and 100,000+ containers with the customer named; the booth notes mark the same engagement "do not reveal customer name". Left off pending confirmation.
