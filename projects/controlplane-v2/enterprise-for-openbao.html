What we do · Enterprise products
Enterprise for OpenBao: secrets management on open foundations.
OpenBao is the Linux Foundation’s open source fork of Vault (MPL-2.0, forked after Vault’s 2023 licence change to BSL 1.1; adopters include Nvidia). We help organisations regain cost control of their secrets management as proprietary tooling becomes increasingly burdensome and restrictive.

04 · Challenge ledger · numbered rows, distinct from service challenge cards
The challenges we hear most
Why secrets estates come under review.
01/
Rising cost and vendor lock-in
Escalating licensing costs and restrictive models limit flexibility, creating long-term dependency on proprietary secrets management platforms.
02/
Fragmented secrets management
Secrets sprawl across agents, tools, pipelines and environments, increasing the risk of exposure, misconfiguration and inconsistent control.
03/
Lack of auditability and compliance assurance
Inability to demonstrate human or non-human identity, access and intent escalates the challenge of meeting regulatory and internal audit requirements.
04/
Operational complexity
Manual processes, inconsistent patterns and a lack of standardisation slow velocity and increase the risk of human error.
05 · Adoption timeline · Assess Design Build Assure as connected steps
Adopting it
A low-risk path from fragmented or proprietary secrets management to OpenBao in production.
Step 01
Assess
We baseline your secrets landscape through security health checks and maturity assessments: sprawl, access risks, cost, compliance gaps and exposure to lock-in.
Step 02
Design
We define a target-state OpenBao architecture using threat modelling and expert-led design, embedding least-privilege access, policy-as-code and auditability.
Step 03
Build
We built OpenBao in the community, and implement it in your platform through cleanroom migration and platform engineering, centralising secrets management.
Step 04
Assure
We validate and operationalise through tabletop exercises and training, strengthening response to credential compromise and audit readiness of controls.
